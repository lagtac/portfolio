§ 01 — Context

Nine years inside a Big Four firm.

I joined KPMG Greece in 2010 and left in 2018. The title was Content Manager on the marketing and digital team; the job was the firm's web estate.

That covered the SharePoint intranet migration, the client and campaign sites, the email and newsletter suite, SEO and analytics, and the pages inside each of them. What a page said and how it worked were one job there: I wrote the content, built the templates, and measured the result.

The nine projects below are a selection made for evidence, not a complete list.

§ 03 — What it shows

Content, web, and marketing were one job.

The work ran from the words on a page to the template that rendered them and the analytics that measured the result. The same combination showed up in the later platform work: one person owning the interface, the data behind it, and the evidence that it held up.

Most of the outcomes are qualitative, because the archive records what was delivered, not what it moved. Where a number exists, it counts the work: 4,913 SEO violations triaged into a documented roadmap on the job board, three annual cycles of the Fortune survey pages, and four campaign families in the email suite.

No brand, no metric. KPMG and its clients appear only as the employer and the work. No KPMG or client brand, screenshot, credential, or measured result is reproduced here; the inventory behind this list stays private.

§ 04 — Direction

The current chapter is JavaScript.

The 2010–2018 work is the foundation: content, web, email, SEO, and brand inside a large firm. The most recent work moved from the page to the service — an annotation tool, a dataset pipeline, a model server, and a clinician app, built and deployed by one developer. The numbers are in the oral pathology case study.

The next step is TypeScript, React, and Next.js. The method does not change: I own the whole job and I keep the evidence real.
