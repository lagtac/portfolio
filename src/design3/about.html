The work has always had the same shape: decide what the system should say, then build every part of it — the schema, the server, the interface, and the deploy that puts it in front of someone.

How I got here

I studied philosophy in Thessaloniki, then a cross-discipline master's in cognitive science in Athens. The thesis was on methods in cognitive musicology: how you study the way people make sense of music, and what a method can show. The habit survived: a claim is only as good as the method behind it.

My first job was EPIS, in 2004: ASP and PHP against SQL Server 2000, including the digital archive of the Greek National Opera. Five years, every layer of the delivery.

Then nine years at KPMG Greece, 2010–2018. The title said Content Manager. The job was the firm's web estate — the intranet migration, the client sites, the email suite, and the pages inside them. What a page said and how it worked were one job there. The KPMG page collects the nine projects and what each one shows.

In 2019 I founded Faltso, a music and theatre group for children, and ran it until 2024: the programmes, the collaborators, the schools and parents' associations, and the site. A small organisation is also a system, and most of its failures are communication failures. The site survives as its repository, 76 commits of it.

Since 2025 I have been the only developer on a four-service clinical imaging platform: annotation tool, dataset pipeline, model server, clinician app. I built it with agentic coding — Claude Code, parallel agents across the repositories, vendor and custom skills — and with the discipline that makes that safe: one contract per boundary, a test for every claim, and a diff I read myself. The case study has the numbers and the three things that did not work.

All four jobs put the value in the same place: between the parts. The annotation tool and the training set; the page and what it does. Systems that can be inspected — run IDs, dated reports, explicit contracts — keep that boundary honest after the context is gone.

I live in Greece with my wife and son. I am looking for a fullstack role where the problem spans more than one layer and the evidence is expected to be real.
